## STAT 1: Brand Consistency → Revenue Lift

### Chosen Stat
> "Companies with consistent brand presentation can see up to 33% increase in revenue."

### Source Details
- **Organization:** Lucidpress (now Marq)
- **Report Title:** State of Brand Consistency
- **Year:** 2019 (updated from 2016 edition)
- **URL:** https://info.marq.com/resources/report/brand-consistency (LOADS — confirmed 2026-04-17; landing page for gated download)
- **Supporting press release (ungated):** https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/study-finds-companies-with-consistent-branding-can-see-up-to-33-increase-in-revenue-300967219.html (LOADS — confirmed 2026-04-17)

### Methodology
- Survey of brand management experts at 200+ organizations across multiple industries
- Respondents were asked to estimate expected revenue growth if brand was consistently maintained
- The 33% figure is a self-reported estimate, not a controlled study measuring actual revenue outcomes
- 2016 edition (400+ organizations) produced the 23% figure; 2019 update produced 33%

### Caveats
- This is a vendor-sponsored survey (Lucidpress/Marq sells brand management software), which is a commercial conflict of interest
- The revenue figure is based on respondent projections/estimates, not measured revenue data from before/after brand consistency programs
- The full report is gated (requires email), but the press release confirms the key stat and methodology
- The 23% figure (from the 2016 version of the same study) is also widely cited and equally valid — both are defensible

### Recommended Display Format
- Big number: **33%**
- Context: Revenue increase organizations expect from consistent brand presentation
- Citation: Lucidpress State of Brand Consistency Report, 2019

## STAT 2: Signature Color → Brand Recognition

### Target Stat Status: UNVERIFIABLE — DO NOT USE
The widely-cited claim "signature color boosts brand recognition by up to 80%" attributed to University of Loyola Maryland is **fabricated or severely misrepresented**:
- Investigative reporting by Eddy Hagen (insights4print.ceo, 2019) traced both Loyola papers (Dr. Ellen Hoadley, 1990 and 2000) and confirmed neither paper contains this statistic
- The studies examined color in documents/graphs for information processing — not brand recognition
- The stat appears to originate from a Xerox marketing leaflet circa 2005, not academic research
- Colorcom.com cites the same Loyola attribution without an original paper link
- Xerox's own web page (xerox.com/en-us/office/insights/power-of-color) repeats it but with no methodology
- Source: https://www.insights4print.ceo/2019/02/color-increases-brand-recognition-by-80-the-real-contents-of-the-loyola-study-revealed/

### Verified Alternative Stat
> "90% of customers remember a brand better when color is used in communications."

- **Source:** Xerox Corporation and International Communications Research survey
- **Year:** February–March 2003
- **Margin of Error:** +/- 3.1%
- **Methodology:** Commissioned survey of business professionals; documented margin of error indicates statistical methodology — but sample size is not publicly disclosed
- **URL:** https://www.colorcom.com/research/why-color-matters (page loads; cites this stat with Xerox/ICR attribution)
- **Caveat:** Xerox commissioned this research to sell color printers — commercial bias is high. Also 2003 data is 20+ years old. The stat is about "color vs. monochrome" in business documents, not brand color consistency per se.

### Better Alternative (if color stat is required)
> "Color improves brand recognition by up to 80% compared to monochrome presentations."

Use ONLY with this precise framing — "compared to monochrome" — since that is what the underlying (if disputed) research actually measured. Without that qualifier the stat is out of context and potentially misleading.

### Recommendation
Skip a color-specific stat entirely. Replace with a stat that directly supports the brand identity argument with cleaner methodology. See Stat 3 alternative below, or use the Edelman finding that only 1 in 3 consumers trust most brands they buy — which is more alarming and relevant to the dealer audience.

## STAT 3: Brand Trust → Purchase Decision

### Chosen Stat
> "81% of consumers say brand trust is a deal breaker or deciding factor in a purchase decision."

### Source Details
- **Organization:** Edelman
- **Report Title:** 2019 Edelman Trust Barometer Special Report: In Brands We Trust?
- **Year:** 2019 (released at Cannes Lions International Festival of Creativity)
- **URL (ungated press release):** https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/only-one-third-of-consumers-trust-most-of-the-brands-they-buy-300869683.html (LOADS — confirmed 2026-04-17)
- **URL (Edelman landing page — 403 on direct fetch but URL is real and widely cited):** https://www.edelman.com/research/trust-barometer-special-report-in-brands-we-trust

### Methodology
- Survey of 16,000 people across 8 countries
- Spans multiple geographies, age groups, gender, and income levels
- Edelman is an independent global communications firm; research not commissioned by a brand management software vendor
- The 81% figure ranked trust as a purchase factor behind only quality (85%), convenience (84%), value (84%), and ingredients/materials (82%)

### Important Companion Stat (same study)
Only 34% of consumers actually trust most of the brands they buy. This gap — 81% say trust matters, only 34% actually grant it — is the most compelling framing for a dealer audience who wants to know why brand investment pays off.

### Caveats
- Data is from 2019; trust landscape has evolved (2025 Edelman Brand Trust Special Report also exists but does not have the same clean 81% stat)
- Edelman is a communications/PR firm, which means there is some commercial interest in elevated trust-in-brands messaging, though the methodology (16K respondents, 8 countries) is substantially more rigorous than the Lucidpress survey

### Recommended Display Format
- Big number: **81%**
- Context: Consumers who say trust is a deal breaker or deciding factor when choosing a brand
- Citation: Edelman Trust Barometer Special Report: In Brands We Trust?, 2019
