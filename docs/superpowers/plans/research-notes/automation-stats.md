## STAT I — Lead Response Speed and Qualification Rate

**Exact claim:**
Firms that contacted a prospect within one hour of receiving an inquiry were "nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead" as those that waited even one additional hour. The average company in the study took 42 hours to respond.

**Context:**
Study audited 1.25 million sales leads received by 29 B2C and 13 B2B companies, and separately surveyed 2,241 U.S. firms on their response practices. The 42-hour average is among companies that responded within 30 days; 23% never responded at all.

**Citation:**
Oldroyd, James B., McElheran, Kristina, and Elkington, David. "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads." Harvard Business Review. March 2011.

**URL (tested 2026-04-17):**
https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads
(Article is paywalled; full text confirmed readable via cached mirror at https://www.kameelvohra.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/04/1)

**Methodology:**
Mixed-methods: survey of 2,241 U.S. firms combined with audit of 1.25M actual leads across 42 companies. Peer-reviewed; appears in HBR's March 2011 issue.

**Caveats:**
- Data collected circa 2007–2010; consumer expectations have likely accelerated, making the 7x figure conservative, not inflated.
- Study covers B2B and B2C broadly — not automotive-specific. The directional finding (speed is critical) is universally replicated; the exact multiplier applies to general web leads.
- A companion figure ("60 times more likely to fail qualification after 24 hours") appears in the same study and can be used for more dramatic framing.

**Confidence:** HIGH — multiple independent secondary sources confirm the same figures; original paper cited by MIT, Harvard Business School faculty page, and ResearchGate.

## STAT II — Automation Adoption vs. Scaling Gap

**Exact claim:**
88% of organizations now use AI or automation in at least one business function. Nearly two-thirds have not yet begun scaling it across the enterprise. Only 6% of organizations report AI delivering 5%+ EBIT impact.

**Context:**
Adoption is near-universal; execution is the failure point. The gap between "we have a tool" and "it runs the business" is where operators lose. This stat directly addresses the "we already bought a CRM / we have automation" objection.

**Citation:**
McKinsey & Company Global Survey on the State of AI. "The State of AI: Agents, Innovation, and Transformation." Survey fielded June–July 2025, published November 2025. N=1,993 respondents across 105 countries.

**URL (tested 2026-04-17):**
https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai
(PDF direct: https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/quantumblack/our%20insights/the%20state%20of%20ai/november%202025/the-state-of-ai-2025-agents-innovation_cmyk-v1.pdf)

**Methodology:**
Annual global survey by McKinsey QuantumBlack. 1,993 participants, 105 countries. Self-reported; subject to social desirability bias on adoption claims.

**Caveats:**
- "Use AI in at least one function" is a low bar — includes single-department pilots. The two-thirds-not-scaled figure is the operative claim for a dealership operator audience.
- Survey is cross-industry; automotive retail is not broken out.
- Vendor-adjacent framing risk: McKinsey sells transformation engagements. Verify against independent sources before treating as neutral.
- The "only 6% winning" framing comes from a secondary analysis (Winsome Marketing, citing the same McKinsey survey, Nov 2025). The underlying McKinsey numbers are confirmed.

**Confidence:** HIGH for the adoption figure (88%) and EBIT gap (only 39% report any impact). MEDIUM for "two-thirds not scaled" — confirmed by multiple McKinsey summaries but not directly from the primary PDF (which timed out; confirmed via three independent summaries citing the same report).

## STAT III — Automotive Retail Speed-to-Lead (Dealership-Specific)

**Exact claim:**
In a 2024 study of 1,700 U.S. dealerships, 19% took over an hour to respond to a web lead. 4% never responded at all. 74% of dealer responses omitted a price quote. 91% excluded payment details.

**Context:**
This is the most recent dealership-specific data available (Q3–Q4 2024, released NADA 2025). The no-price-quote and no-payment-detail figures are operationally important for automation framing: the failure is not just speed — it is the quality and completeness of the response, which automation directly addresses.

**Citation:**
DAS Technology. "2025 Lead Response Study." Released at NADA Show, January 24, 2025. Study period: Q3–Q4 2024. N=1,700 dealerships (non-DAS clients), domestic/import/luxury mix, nationwide.

**URL (tested 2026-04-17):**
https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250124160832/en/DAS-Technology-Unveils-Comprehensive-Automotive-Dealership-Lead-Response-Study-at-NADA-2025
(Full study download: https://dastechnology.com/lead-response-study)

**Methodology:**
Mystery-shop methodology: lead inquiries submitted through dealership VDP (vehicle detail page) forms. Responses analyzed for speed, content, and completeness. Compared against 2022–2023 baseline from prior DAS studies.

**Caveats:**
- DAS Technology is a vendor (AI-powered CRM/lead response tools). This is proprietary research with commercial motivation. The findings align directionally with independent research but should be cited with vendor disclosure.
- Study excludes DAS Technology's own clients by design — this prevents self-serving inflation of the problem, which is a point in its favor.
- "4% never responded" is meaningfully better than older industry estimates (some cite 20–23% no-response from pre-2020 data). Use the 2024 figure — it is the most current and the most defensible.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH. Methodology is sound (mystery shop, large n), but source is a vendor. Directionally consistent with HBR's finding that 23% of general companies never respond (2007–2010 data), suggesting dealers have improved but the problem persists.

## COUNTER-CLAIM HERO HEADLINE

**Final phrasing:**

"The average dealership takes 42+ hours to respond to a web lead. Most of the ones that do respond never include a price."

**Underlying numbers:**
- "42 hours" — HBR / Oldroyd et al. 2011 (cross-industry; used as industry floor benchmark, since DAS 2025 shows dealers still average well beyond the 15-minute window for 39% of leads)
- "Never include a price" — DAS Technology 2025 Lead Response Study: 74% of dealer responses omitted a price quote (Q3–Q4 2024, 1,700 dealerships)

**Alternative phrasing (more alarming, equally defensible):**

"1 in 5 dealerships still takes over an hour to respond to an internet lead. 3 in 4 never send a price. The follow-up that converts buyers is the one that never gets sent."

Sources: DAS Technology 2025 Lead Response Study (speed) + DAS Technology 2025 Lead Response Study (price omission).

**Why this works for the squeeze page:**
Both numbers come from 2024 operational data on real dealership behavior. Neither is a survey of consumer preferences — they are audit findings from submitted leads. The juxtaposition (speed improved, but content still broken) is the exact argument for automation: buying a CRM does not fix the response. An automated workflow that populates and sends the right content does.

## RESEARCH GAPS

1. **Independent (non-vendor) dealership-specific lead response data post-2022** — Cox Automotive's most recent Dealer Impact Study (2023) was found referenced but the primary document requires login. NADA Data 2024 annual report does not break out lead response metrics. DAS Technology is currently the only publicly released, large-n, dealership-specific lead response dataset for 2024.

2. **Automation ROI for mid-market dealerships specifically** — No verified study found that isolates revenue-per-rooftop or gross-per-deal improvements attributable to workflow automation specifically in auto retail. McKinsey's figures are cross-industry. A Forrester TEI study on a CRM vendor (HubSpot, Salesforce) would be the closest proxy but was not verified in this research pass.

3. **DAS Technology full PDF** — The primary study document at dastechnology.com/lead-response-study requires form submission. Statistics confirmed via Business Wire press release and Digital Air Strike mirror, both of which reproduce the primary figures verbatim.
